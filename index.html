ABOUT

About the Project

This project studies how renewable-energy sites can be selected using optimization methods.

Methodology

Real data → binary variables → QUBO → 5-qubit QAOA → simulation → result

Scope

The example uses 5 candidate regions and selects exactly 2 regions for maximum annual wind + solar generation.

Limitation

This is a classical simulation of QAOA for demonstration and learning purposes. This is a proof-of-concept and does not claim quantum advantage.

01 — THE PROBLEM

Why renewable-energy portfolio selection is an optimization problem.

The challenge isn't just generating clean energy, but coordinating resources. This involves balancing solar and wind, managing battery storage, predicting demand, and respecting grid constraints like budget, generation potential, geographic variation, transmission capacities, and available storage.

Key questions arise: Which sites to develop? How much power to generate? When to charge/discharge? As the scale increases, this becomes a combinatorial search space.

Research Question

"Can quantum optimization methods such as QAOA remain useful as renewable-energy planning problems scale to hundreds or thousands of decision variables?"

Why Optimization Is Needed

For n=5 regions picking k=2, the total combinations C(5,2) = 10 is trivial to brute-force classically. This is a proof-of-concept for scalability. However, this research explores formulation and algorithmic scaling. As N grows (e.g., micro-grids across hundreds of districts), the combinatorial space explodes. We investigate if the QUBO formulation and QAOA solver can efficiently handle this constrained search space.

Decision Variables

We introduce x₁ to x₅ as binary variables where 0 = not selected and 1 = selected.

x₁: Rajasthan x₂: Gujarat x₃: Karnataka x₄: Tamil Nadu x₅: Andhra Pradesh

Exactly two regions must be selected.

Maximize total energy yield (Eᵢ).

03 — HOW THE PROBLEM BECOMES A QUANTUM OPTIMIZATION PROBLEM

QUBO converts the selection objective and constraint into a form that QAOA can optimize. Pipeline: Real Data (GWh/year) -> Binary Model (xᵢ=0,1) -> QUBO (Objective + Penalty) -> Qubits (5 qubits) -> QAOA (Layers) -> Measure (Probabilities).

Cost Function C(x)
C(x) = - Σ Eixi + λ ( Σ xi - 2 )2
trending_up Reward (Objective Term)

Minimizing negative yield is equivalent to maximizing yield. Eᵢ is the total energy for region i.

gavel Penalty Term

Enforces the constraint. If sum(xᵢ) ≠ 2, a massive penalty (λ) is added, raising the energy state far above the ground state.

Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm

QAOA is a hybrid quantum-classical algorithm: the circuit is quantum, while a classical optimizer tunes its parameters. It uses alternating layers of a Cost Hamiltonian (derived from our QUBO) and a Mixer Hamiltonian to evolve an initial superposition state towards the optimal ground state. The classical optimizer adjusts γ and β to minimize the expected value of the Cost Hamiltonian.

Current Limitations

  • Noisy Hardware: Real quantum hardware introduces decoherence. Our results are from a noise-free simulator.
  • Small Instance: For p=2, QAOA on dense MAXCUT-like QUBOs does not currently guarantee supremacy over advanced classical heuristics. No speedup claimed. n=5 is a small proof of concept.
  • Richer Constraints Needed: Real grids require more complex constraints than just selecting 2 regions.
  • Simulation Used: Results rely on classical state-vector simulation.
